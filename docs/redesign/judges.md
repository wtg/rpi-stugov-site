# Judge verdicts

## Student lens

### Concept 1: The Front Door (task-first services) — 8/10

**Strengths**
- Passes the under-a-minute test for three of the four student personas. A first-year taps 'Raise an issue' and picks a topic. A club treasurer taps 'Get club or travel funding' and lands on a start page that says what they need and how long it takes. The 'Senate passed something' student taps 'See what was decided'. No knowledge of the Executive Board or Grand Marshal is needed.
- Verb-labelled six-item stacked list above the fold works on a phone with one thumb, with no hero image or carousel to scroll past.
- The ServicePage 'before you start' block (what you need, deadline, how long a decision takes) answers the club treasurer's real anxiety: am I too late, and will I get the money in time?
- Body pages lead with one plain sentence and a 'What you can do here' list. This is the only concept where the org chart is translated for someone who has never heard of it.
- The stale-banner-plus-role-email fallback means a student never hits a dead end like today's 404 complaint form.
- 'This week' collapses to 'No meetings scheduled this week' instead of showing February events in October.

**Fatal flaws**
- It risks fake doors. If club funding is actually run by Union staff or Club Financial Advisors through another system, 'Request club contingency funding' is a nicely written mailto. The student finds out only after clicking. Do not ship any ServicePage until the RUGP and constitution confirm a real process and owner.
- The 'Get help' versus 'Get funding and space' versus 'Your representatives' split is still guesswork for a first-year. 'Get help' sounds like counselling or IT. Rename it to 'Raise an issue' and test the labels with real students.
- 'Speak at a meeting' is in the top six, but almost no student does it. It takes a slot better used by 'Find events' or 'Join a club/committee'.
- Weakest for the 'what passed' student. 'See what was decided' points to a decisions archive that is Box-backed and has no plain-language summary, so the student lands on a list of PDFs named like '#12 07_30_26'.

### Concept 2: The Public Record (transparency & legislation) — 6/10

**Strengths**
- Best for the 'I heard the Senate passed something' student. Keyword search returns a row such as 'PASSED · Extends library hours during finals · Oct 19' with a one-sentence plain summary. That answers it in about 15 seconds.
- 'No status is ever blank' ('Minutes not posted, 18 days') is honest and builds trust for the students who do check.
- Decisions have their own URLs, so a decision can be dropped into Discord or Instagram. That is how most students will actually arrive.
- Allocations link to the vote that approved them, which gives a club treasurer real precedent ('clubs like mine got $X last time').

**Fatal flaws**
- A first-year on a phone is greeted by a search box, a meeting card and a list of decision IDs. Nothing on that screen says what they can do for themselves. It reads like C-SPAN, and they will not come back.
- Jargon leaks through: identifiers like 'S57-014', stage labels like 'Response', 'Special Order', and 'Club Contingencies' all need translating. 'Contingency' means nothing to a student. Say 'extra club funding you can apply for during the year'.
- The grad student with a stipend or housing concern has nowhere obvious to go. 'Have your say' is the sixth nav item and is just a form.
- On launch it has zero decisions. An empty tracker is worse than none, and the concept depends on backfilling minutes that nobody has scheduled.

### Concept 3: Your Representatives (representation & feedback loop) — 6.5/10

**Strengths**
- The 'Who handles what' topic router ('Dining is bad → RLDC') is the single most student-useful idea across all four concepts. It answers 'who do I tell?' without the student learning any committee names.
- Strongest for the grad student. /your-reps/graduate/ shows the Graduate Senators and the Graduate Class Rep with an office-hours and contact button, and the issue is routed to a Role, so it survives senator turnover.
- 'You said, we did' entries with an owner Role and a dated status are the only thing in any concept that gives a student a reason to come back.
- Shareable, no-login URLs (/your-reps/2028/) work in a class GroupMe or Discord.
- It handles vacancies honestly: 'Seat open: here's how it gets filled' turns an empty card into a recruitment pitch.

**Fatal flaws**
- The homepage opens with 'I'm a…' and rep cards, but most students do not want to meet their senator. They want the dining problem fixed. The chooser sits in front of the task.
- The Class of 2030 trap: the July agenda lists Senate seats only for '27 to '29. A first-year who picks '30 in September sees an empty or 'not yet elected' page on their very first visit. That is the worst possible first impression for the exact persona who most needs the site.
- The club treasurer is badly served. Funding is a secondary destination, and 'Clubs & organizations' under Your reps is not where anyone would look for money.
- It depends on SCEC triaging an inbox weekly. If they don't, the visible 'Working on it' wall reads as spin, and moderation invites censorship complaints.

### Concept 4: The Operator's Console (built for the people who run it) — 5/10

**Strengths**
- Students benefit indirectly and a lot. The next-meeting card always has the full date and year and an honest 'Agenda not posted yet', and stale content drops off instead of lying. That fixes today's worst failures: eight-month-old events and 404 links.
- Rollover leaves seats Vacant rather than showing last year's names. A student emailing a senator who graduated is a real, common failure, and this prevents it.
- Collapsing empty sections instead of showing 'No upcoming events' keeps the phone homepage short.

**Fatal flaws**
- Its value is invisible to students. The student-facing layer is borrowed from the other concepts, so from this lens it cannot be scored higher than they are.
- Its own homepage gives the meeting card the top slot. Meetings are the government's priority, not the student's. A first-year does not know why they would attend a Senate meeting.
- 'Recently decided' with stage chips like 'Introduced → In committee → Voted → Response' is procedure-speak, not plain language.
- Get help is the fourth nav item and Documents is a top-level nav item. A first-year would never pick Documents, so a nav slot is wasted on insiders.

**Best ideas across concepts**
- The topic router (Concept 3) as the core of 'Raise an issue'. Use plain questions ('Problem with dining or housing?'), each mapped to an owner Role, and include an 'outside student government' note such as 'RPI Dining runs the meal plan; RLDC pushes them'. It must include grad-specific topics (stipend, grad housing, TA workload) that route to the Graduate Council and Graduate Senators, not only to the Senate committees.
- A task-first stacked list of verbs above the fold (Concept 1), limited to what students actually come for: Get money for my club, Raise an issue, Find out what passed, Who represents me, Find events, Run for office or join a committee. Drop 'Speak at a meeting' from the top six and put it inside the meeting card.
- A plain-language glossary applied everywhere, with no unexplained term on any student-facing page. 'Grand Marshal' needs a one-line explanation (RPI-specific, and confusing to every first-year). 'Contingency' becomes 'extra funding clubs can request during the year'. 'Special Ballroom Request' becomes 'Book the Union Ballroom for an event'. 'Invitation to Speak' becomes 'Anyone can speak at the start and end of each meeting'.
- Plain-summary decision rows with shareable URLs (Concept 2), searchable by keyword. Label status as 'Passed', 'Failed', 'Waiting on a vote', or 'Done: here's what changed', never 'Response' or 'Special Order'.
- A start page for funding (Concept 1). Up front it says who can apply, the deadline, what you need, how long a decision takes, and how much is left this year (the four budget lines, as of a date). That last fact is the one number a club treasurer actually wants.
- 'You said, we did' entries owned by a Role, with a dated status (Concepts 1 and 3). Launch only with real, closed items, including honest 'Not possible, here's why' outcomes, so the page shows results rather than spin.
- A first-year fallback on the reps view. When a constituency has no elected seats yet (Class of 2030 in early fall), show 'Your class elects senators in [month]. Until then, these people represent everyone:' with the Grand Marshal, the President and the at-large members. Never show an empty page.
- Every date includes the year, and empty sections collapse or state what they mean (Concepts 2 and 4). This directly fixes 'No upcoming events' and RPI's year-less events.
- Design for arrival from Discord or Instagram, not from the homepage. Every decision, issue, meeting and reps view gets a stable, readable URL and a sensible link preview.
- 'Is this page useful? / Report a problem' on every page, sent to a shared inbox. It is cheap and gives students a feedback channel even on reference pages.

**Recommendation**

Prototype Concept 1 (Front Door) as the student-facing shell. Its 'Raise an issue' path should be Concept 3's topic router, not a generic form. Its 'Find out what passed' path should be a lean version of Concept 2's decision list: plain summaries, keyword search, shareable URLs and plain status words. From a student's point of view, Concept 1 is the only one where a first-year who has never heard of the Executive Board can finish a task in under a minute. The club treasurer's funding need is first-class, and the 'Senate passed something' and grad-concern cases are each one tap away once those two borrowed pieces are in. Concept 4 is the right engine underneath (terms, vacancy-on-rollover, freshness, collapse-when-empty), but students should never see it. Concept 3's rep chooser should be a homepage section below the task list, not the front door, and it needs the Class of 2030 fallback.

Build two homepage variants in /lab/ on the same data, and put each in front of three or four real students on a phone with the four persona tasks. Variant A is a verb list first, then 'This week', then reps. Variant B is 'Raise an issue / Get money' plus the topic router up front, with the decision search second. Time each task and note where they hesitate on wording. Before writing any start page for funding or room booking, confirm against the constitution and the RUGP that the process and its owner really exist. A polished start page that ends in a guessed email address is the one failure that would cost student trust faster than today's 404.

## Maintainer lens

### Concept 4: The Operator's Console — 8/10

**Strengths**
- It is the only concept that treats publishing effort as the design problem. The two-minute rule and 'an agenda PDF alone is a valid publish' match how a weekly secretary works. AgendaItems are optional, so the minimum workload is 'paste a Box link'.
- Meeting is modelled as a snippet with a routable index (/meetings/senate/57/12/). That keeps dozens of meeting pages out of the Wagtail page tree, and avoids the per-page draft/publish overhead that a secretary would grind through every week.
- Decision history comes from the AgendaItems that reference it. The secretary never logs the same action twice, which is the drift that Cornell's meeting table shows.
- Term plus assignment start/end/confirmed_on, together with rollover_term leaving seats Vacant, fixes the worst failure at turnover: last year's names shown as current. It also replaces Role.constituency, which is currently computed from the current year, with an explicit term key.
- Notices must carry an expiry date (14-day cap) and the homepage has no StreamField. That leaves almost no hand-written content on the front page that can rot. If nobody touches the site for a year, the worst case is honest.
- It reuses the most existing code: the Role/Member trio, the BoxFileCache/YYYY-MM-DD matching, OIDC group mapping per role, the ComplaintFormPage engine, and EventPage recurrence.
- The Desk via construct_homepage_panels is a cheap way to make handoff a to-do list instead of a wiki, if it stays read-only queries.

**Fatal flaws**
- Auto-generating meetings from a recurrence rule will create phantom meetings. The evidence already contradicts a fixed schedule: sg.rpi.edu says the Senate meets Mondays at 7pm, but the #12 agenda is Thursday Jul 30 at 8pm, and the EBoard met Tuesday Aug 25 at 7pm. Generated meetings that never happened would then publicly show 'Agenda not posted', which is false. Replace this with a 'create next meeting (prefilled from the last one)' button.
- The custom 'Post agenda' admin view, the Desk escalation logic, generate_meetings, rollover_term and stale_report are all custom code. A volunteer developer leaving in 2 years cannot maintain all of it, and events/records currently have zero tests. Ship only snippet forms with good defaults plus read-only homepage panels. Add custom views only after secretaries have actually used the forms.
- Editing inline AgendaItem orderables in the Wagtail admin on a phone is clumsy. 'Two minutes from a phone' holds for the PDF-only path, not for structured items.
- The Decision stages, numbering and term boundaries are guessed without the constitution. Migrating a wrong schema later is the most expensive mistake in this concept.

### Concept 3: Your Representatives — 6/10

**Strengths**
- It has the cheapest build on this codebase. It mostly extends Role/MemberProfile/MemberRoleAssignment, and members.csv/roles.csv already hold the real roster once the importers are fixed.
- Constituency pages generated from Role records decay well. With no maintenance they still show seats, 'Vacant', and the body's shared email.
- Issues owned by a Role (not a person) let open items pass to the next seat holder at turnover.
- /your-reps/2028/ works without JS and can be shared, which avoids accessibility and performance risk. The localStorage remembered choice is a progressive enhancement only.
- The Topic router snippet (Dining goes to RLDC, Buildings to FSC) is static, low-churn data that hardly decays.

**Fatal flaws**
- The Issue/IssueUpdate loop creates a new standing job: SCEC weekly triage, moderation, publish decisions and outcome writing. Moderation invites accusations of censorship. When SCEC turns over or loses interest, the flagship section becomes a wall of 'No update since…'.
- Asking each of about 50 reps to confirm their profile and office hours every semester is a recurring task spread across many people. In practice most profiles will show 'may be out of date' within a term.
- The secretary's core weekly job, agendas and minutes, is reduced to a 'Box-backed list'. The people who publish most often get the least from it.
- The constituency model needs real rework before anything works. Role.constituency is single-valued and computed from the current year, the 7 'Greek & Independent' seats are mixed, and students belong to more than one constituency. None of it is checked against the constitution.
- Rep photos and emails need opt-in, so cards will be uneven from launch.

### Concept 1: The Front Door — 5/10

**Strengths**
- Assigning ownership by Role (owner_role on every service page) plus last_reviewed/review_every_days with a stale banner, and removal from the homepage at twice the interval, is the right decay mechanism.
- The homepage alert slot expires automatically, and the 'This week' cards are generated, so the front page cannot show old meetings as upcoming.
- Using ComplaintFormPage as the engine for form-backed services reuses working code.
- The keyboard-operable nav replacement and 'no JS except the picker' are low accessibility and performance risk.

**Fatal flaws**
- ServicePage and StepByStepPage are hand-written plain-language prose. It is the content type that decays fastest and depends most on the writer, and the concept admits this. Within a year of turnover, the 120-day review banners will cover most of the site.
- Fake-door risk is high. Nobody has confirmed who owns club or travel funding, room requests or club registration (likely Union staff, Club Financial Advisors or RUGP processes elsewhere). Building start pages before reading the constitution and RUGP means publishing processes that may not exist.
- The IssueRouter plus Outcome loop needs chairs to respond within 21 days and publish consent handling. That is a burden similar to Concept 3's issue loop, with less structure behind it.
- The page types are the most numerous: ServicePage, ServiceIndexPage, StepByStep, IssueRouter, MeetingPage and snippets. That is a lot of templates for the next maintainer to learn, for maybe six real services.

### Concept 2: The Public Record — 4/10

**Strengths**
- 'No status is ever blank' and the computed badges (minutes overdue, budget as-of date) make neglect visible instead of misleading.
- Its decay floor is good: if nobody enters motions, meeting pages still carry the Box agenda and minutes links, which already beats a bare Box folder.
- The BudgetLine snippet with an as-of date and a source document is a cheap widget. The treasurer copies four numbers already printed on every EBoard agenda.
- The Term snippet as the key for institutional memory is right.

**Fatal flaws**
- Making Decision a Wagtail Page means the secretary creates, edits and publishes a separate page per motion, with tree placement and revisions, plus separate DecisionAction rows that repeat what the meeting already records. That is the highest publishing effort of the four, and it will be skipped.
- Linking each Allocation to the decision that approved it, roll-call votes and attendance add more data entry and political friction. Roll-call votes need a Senate rules decision before they can be built at all.
- Hiding the tracker until 10 records exist means launch depends on a backfill of past minutes by someone who will not do it. An empty or hidden tracker at launch repeats the UT/Houston failure.
- It has the largest guessed schema (stages, numbering, Term roster_snapshot as frozen JSON, Allocation), all without the constitution. A mis-modelled schema is the costliest thing for a volunteer developer to migrate later.
- The Term.roster_snapshot JSON duplicates what assignment start/end dates already give, and it can drift from them.

**Best ideas across concepts**
- Make the meeting the publishing unit. A meeting is valid with only body, term, number, date/time, location or Webex link, and a Box agenda URL. Minutes URL and status get added later. AgendaItems and Decisions are optional extras (C4/C2).
- Model Meeting and Decision as snippets with a routable index, not as Wagtail Pages. Create a Decision inline from an AgendaItem and derive its history from the AgendaItems that reference it. Do not keep a separate action log (C4 over C2).
- Instead of recurrence-generated meetings, add a 'Create next meeting' action that copies location, time and section template from the previous meeting. The real schedule moves around (Thu 8pm, Tue 7pm, and 'Mondays 7pm' on the live site).
- Add a Freshness mixin (owner_role, reviewed_on, review_interval). It shows a stale banner past the interval and removes the item from the homepage at twice the interval. Apply it only to the few hand-written pages (C1/C4).
- Add Notice/alert entries with a required expiry date of at most 14 days. Keep the homepage free of StreamField so it is built entirely from queries plus one expiring notice (C4/C1).
- Add start and end dates plus confirmed_on to MemberRoleAssignment, keyed to a Term. Rollover leaves seats Vacant rather than showing last year's names. Replace Role.constituency, which is computed from the current year, with explicit stored values (C4/C3).
- Give each role a shared email that survives turnover, and use it as the fallback contact whenever a profile is stale (C1/C3).
- Build no-JS constituency pages at /your-reps/<key>/ from role assignments, with an optional localStorage shortcut on the homepage (C3).
- Add a static Topic snippet that maps a student problem to a committee, with an 'outside SG' note such as 'RPI Dining runs the meal plan' (C3). It is low-churn and needs no moderation.
- Add a BudgetLine snippet with four lines and an as-of date plus source, updated by the treasurer from the numbers already printed on each EBoard agenda (C2/C4).
- Write honest empty states. 'Agenda not posted yet' or 'Minutes pending (18 days)' is never a blank, and a homepage section with no fresh data collapses instead of saying 'No upcoming events' (C2/C4).
- Add read-only Desk panels via construct_homepage_panels listing overdue minutes, stale pages and the upcoming rollover. Use only queries, with no custom admin views in v1 (C4).
- Put an identifier footer on every page reading 'maintained by WTG, last reviewed <date>', with a 'Report a problem with this page' link to a shared inbox (C1/C2).
- Reduce the homepage to four verbs, and only verbs backed by a real process: Find my rep, Find the next meeting / speak at it, See what was decided, Request funding. The funding verb links to the existing EBoard or staff process until RUGP is read.
- Defer these until the constitution and RUGP are read and secretaries have used the basics: the moderated Issue tracker, roll-call/attendance, Allocation-to-decision links, StepByStep journeys, and more than about 3 ServicePages.
- Handoff hygiene that costs almost nothing: fix import_roles/import_assignments to read the real CSVs, commit or document the Tailwind build, drop RecordPage/BlogPage/AltHome/the hard-coded 404 links, and cap the number of page types.

**Recommendation**

Prototype Concept 4's data model as the engine, with Concept 3's constituency pages as the main student-facing feature and Concept 1's homepage verbs cut down to four. Build it in /lab/ as two prototypes seeded from real data: the two agenda PDFs, members.csv and roles.csv. (a) A public prototype: a homepage built only from queries (next meeting card, four verbs, recently decided, your reps, budget snapshot, one expiring notice), a Cornell-style meeting page for Senate 57 #12, and /your-reps/2028/. (b) An admin-flow prototype using Meeting, AgendaItem and Decision snippets in the stock Wagtail snippet UI, with a 'create next meeting from previous' action. Time how long it takes a secretary to post the #13 agenda (PDF-only path) and record outcomes, on desktop and on a phone. If the PDF-only publish can't be done in about two minutes using stock forms, the model is wrong. Fix that before writing any custom admin views.

Explicitly do not build in this round: recurrence-generated meetings (the schedule is visibly irregular), custom Post-agenda or Desk views beyond read-only homepage panels, Decision-as-Page, the moderated Issue loop, roll-call votes, and ServicePages for processes whose owners are unconfirmed. Concept 2's full record and Concept 1's start pages should wait until someone reads the constitution and RUGP, and until the secretaries have kept the simple meeting record current for a few weeks. That keeps the custom code to what a successor can learn in an afternoon, and the site degrades to dated Box links rather than to false claims.

## Skeptic lens

### Concept 1: The Front Door (task-first services) — 6/10

**Strengths**
- It is the only concept whose homepage helps a first-year who doesn't know what the Senate is. It leads with verbs and keeps the org chart out of the way.
- It needs the least new structured data. The core is hand-written start pages plus generated meetings, so it can launch without a backfill.
- It admits its own fatal weakness: the Union has about six real services, and padding the list builds fake doors. Holding to 'no real process, no page' is the right discipline.
- Pages are owned by roles, with role email addresses, which survives turnover better than anything tied to a person.
- 'Speak at a meeting' is treated as a service. It explains Invitation to Speak and Press & Visitor Q/A, which need no data and are currently invisible.

**Fatal flaws**
- Its service list comes from two agendas and a guess. Club and Travel Contingency requests, ballroom requests and club registration are probably run through Union staff, Club Financial Advisors or an RUGP process, maybe in another system. Without the constitution and RUGP, three of the six homepage tasks could be well-written redirects to an email address. That is exactly the fake door the concept warns about.
- Stale-content decay is self-defeating. A ServicePage that passes 2x its review interval drops off the homepage task list. Nobody clicks 'reviewed' in the April crunch, so by May the funding link silently leaves the homepage. That is the IA hiding the most important thing because of an admin chore.
- Public 'This may be out of date' banners on a 120-day cycle mean that by spring most hand-written pages carry a warning. The site then looks abandoned, and students learn to ignore the banners. Staleness should be enforced in admin, not shown publicly, except on content that is genuinely time-sensitive.
- IssueRouterPage plus the Outcome snippet is a new moderation and response workflow with no owner. It needs publish consent, anonymisation, and chairs writing outcomes within 21 days. Expect a wall of 'Still being looked at' by week 6.
- The 'Who represents you' picker on the homepage needs Role.constituency to be filled in. The DB has 0 roles, import_roles silently drops roles.csv's constituency columns, and members.csv has no emails. Day one it shows nothing.
- The five bodies sit under 'About the Union'. The Senate, its agendas and J-Board rulings are then two levels deep, behind a label that reads like a history page. Press and officers, who are repeat users, pay for that.

### Concept 2: The Public Record (transparency & legislation) — 4/10

**Strengths**
- Its principles are the most valuable of the four. 'No status is ever blank' and 'one record per thing, every list generated' each answer a failure that actually happened at peer sites (UT's blank cells, Cornell's mismatched tables).
- It makes the Meeting page the unit of transparency. That is the right atom, and it lines up with the fixed agenda order both bodies already use.
- Term as a first-class record, with assignments that end instead of being deleted, is the right way to keep institutional memory.
- Showing an as-of date and a source on every figure means stale numbers look stale rather than wrong.

**Fatal flaws**
- It has the highest data-entry burden and the most guessed schema. A Decision has an identifier, stage, outcome, inline Actions with yes/no/abstain, optional roll-call, sponsors M2M, amounts, related M2M and implementation fields. Neither agenda has a numbered bill, so the identifier format (S57-014) is invented. Tallies only exist if the minutes record them, and nobody has checked that they do. Secretaries who now drop a PDF in Box are being asked to retype minutes into a tracker. They won't.
- The homepage is built on records that don't exist yet. On launch 'Latest decisions' has zero rows and 'You said, we did' (the Implemented filter) has zero rows. The concept's own fix of backfilling the 57th Senate and FY27 from minutes is a big unowned project. Hiding sections until there are 10 records means the homepage is mostly hidden.
- 'Money right now' is misleading. The four lines are contingency pools, not where the student activity fee goes, and on the only data point all four are at 100%. Allocations linked to decisions need Business Ops and Treasurer buy-in. The Digital Infrastructure Plan classes budgets as private docs, so publishing them is a policy decision the site can't make.
- Roll-call votes and attendance are politically explosive inside a 19-seat Senate. The concept itself says it needs a Senate rules decision. Don't build it before that decision is made.
- 'Searchable decisions and archive' is oversold. Wagtail's database search indexes page fields, not Box PDFs, and BoxFileCache holds only filenames. The sync has never run because Box CCG credentials have never been configured. Full text of agendas and minutes stays unsearchable.
- People becomes the bucket for all five bodies. That works for a tracker user but hides 'what does the Senate do' from a casual student.
- The page reads like C-SPAN, which the concept concedes. That matters because students come for money and help.

### Concept 3: Your Representatives (representation & feedback loop) — 5/10

**Strengths**
- A shareable, bookmarkable /your-reps/2028/ URL with no login and no JavaScript is the cheapest real personalisation available. It is generated entirely from Role and assignment records that already exist in the schema.
- Seats render even when empty ('Seat open: here's how it gets filled'), so worst-case decay still tells the truth.
- The topic router ('Dining is bad, who do I tell?' leads to RLDC) is high value and nearly free. Topics map to committees that already exist, and the router needs no outcomes data.
- The 'outside_sg' field ('RPI Dining runs the meal plan; RLDC advocates to them') sets honest expectations about what student government can actually change.

**Fatal flaws**
- The personalisation depends on constituency data the Union doesn't have. The 7 'Greek & Independent' Senate seats split by FSL membership, and students may not know which constituency they're in. Co-terms, 5th-years and transfers don't map cleanly. The '30 Senate seats don't appear on the Jul 30 agenda. Role.constituency is single-valued and computed from the current year. How each seat is actually elected hasn't been checked against the constitution.
- The rep cards will be thin. members.csv has no emails, photos and public emails are opt-in, and most senators don't hold office hours. The structured office_hours and preferred_contact fields will be blank or 'Profile not confirmed' for most seats, which makes it the vanity directory the concept warns about.
- The Issue/IssueUpdate tracker is a new standing workflow handed to SCEC, which the concept calls 'the committee's existing job description' without evidence. It needs weekly triage, moderation, which invites censorship complaints, outcome writing, and per-role updates every 60 days. Without a committed owner it is the first feature to die, and a dead feedback loop does more harm than having none.
- Attaching issues to a Role means a newly elected senator inherits a queue of promises they never made and may oppose. Accountability attached to the seat sounds good, but in practice nobody owns it.
- Funding, ballroom requests and the decision record are demoted to destinations. MIT and KU evidence says money is the main reason students visit. The thesis optimises for a question ('who is my senator?') that most students never ask.

### Concept 4: The Operator's Console (built for the people who run it) — 6/10

**Strengths**
- It is the only concept that treats staleness as the main design problem, which is what every peer site and the current RPI site actually fail at.
- Making terms the key, with a rollover that leaves seats Vacant rather than showing last year's names, fixes the most common visible rot.
- Notice with a required expiry capped at 14 days is the right call. Hand-entered homepage content can't outlive its relevance.
- 'AgendaItems must stay optional; the agenda PDF on its own is a valid publish' is the single most important rule in any of the four concepts.
- The Desk's 'clear your list' framing turns handoff documentation into something used every day rather than a wiki nobody reads.
- A calendar subscription per body lets students stop checking the site at all, which is low-maintenance value.

**Fatal flaws**
- Auto-generating meetings from a recurrence rule will create public falsehoods. The facts already disagree: the live site says Mondays at 7pm, the Jul 30, 2026 Senate meeting was a Thursday at 8pm, and the EBoard met on a Tuesday. Breaks, finals, cancellations and room changes will produce phantom meetings showing 'Agenda not posted' in the permanent public record. That is a shame badge on a meeting that never happened. Meetings should be created by a person, from a template, not generated.
- It has the most custom code for the smallest volunteer team: the Desk panels, a custom Post agenda admin view, generate_meetings, rollover_term, stale_report, and role-driven job lists. Events and records have no tests today. The next WTG chair inherits a bespoke app, which is itself the sustainability failure the concept is trying to prevent.
- The 'two-minute rule from a phone' assumes the Wagtail admin is usable on mobile and that operators have role-scoped logins. OIDC is off by default, the group-claim mapping has never been exercised against real campus groups, and the Desk's 'your jobs by role' needs that mapping working.
- The Decision tracker carries the same guessed-stage and unnumbered-bill problem as Concept 2, deferred rather than solved.
- It becomes cold and database-like if it is the visible personality. The concept concedes the console should be the engine, not the face.

**Best ideas across concepts**
- Fix the broken basics before choosing any concept: /complaint-form/ and /get-involved/ return 404, /the-record/ 302-redirects to Box and hides every RecordPage, the homepage quick links are empty, the nav is hover-only and not keyboard-accessible, and events have no year. All four concepts assume these get fixed, and doing it is uncontroversial.
- Make the Meeting page the unit of transparency (Concepts 2 and 4). The required fields are body, term, date and time with the year, location or Webex link, and the Box links for agenda and minutes. AgendaItems and outcomes are optional enrichment. The agenda PDF on its own is a complete publish.
- Never leave a status blank. 'Agenda not posted', 'Minutes not yet posted (meeting was 18 days ago)', 'Unknown' and 'Vacant' are explicit values, never empty cells (Concepts 2 and 4).
- Key assignments to terms, with term_start and term_end, and have rollover leave seats Vacant instead of deleting rows or showing last year's names (Concepts 2, 3 and 4). This is the cheapest anti-rot measure available.
- /your-reps/<constituency>/ as a shareable, login-free, JavaScript-free generated URL (Concept 3). It is only viable once import_roles is fixed to read roles.csv's constituency_class and constituency_fsl columns, which it currently drops silently.
- A static topic-to-committee router ('Dining or housing → RLDC', 'Buildings → FSC', 'Academics → AAC'), with an 'outside student government' note and a role email address. It works with zero outcome tracking, so build it and skip the issue tracker (Concept 3).
- A 'How to speak at a meeting' explainer for the standing Invitation to Speak and Press & Visitor Q/A items. It needs no data, has real value, and nobody explains these items today (Concepts 1 and 3).
- Notice or alert slot with a required expiry date capped at 14 days (Concept 4).
- Role-owned pages that contact role or shared email addresses, never personal ones (Concepts 1 and 3).
- Show staleness in admin (a Wagtail report or homepage panel), not on public pages. Public stale banners only go on content that is genuinely time-sensitive, such as funding deadlines and election dates.
- Start pages in the GOV.UK pattern only for processes confirmed in the constitution or RUGP, probably at most 3 or 4 (funding, ballroom request, run for office). Never pad the list (Concept 1's own rule, applied strictly).
- A per-body calendar subscription feed (ICS) built on the existing EventPage recurrence code. Students get meeting changes pushed to them without visiting the site (Concept 4).
- A footer identifier: 'Part of the Rensselaer Union · maintained by the Web Technologies Group · last updated date' (all four concepts).
- One name everywhere, to end the drift between 'Union', 'RPI Student Government' and 'Student Senate' (Concept 1).
- A 'How this site works' handoff page with a rollover checklist (all four concepts). This is cheaper and more durable than a custom Desk.
- Treat these as secretly the same idea. Concepts 2 and 4 share one data model (Meeting, AgendaItem, Decision, Term, BudgetLine) and differ only in Concept 4's admin tooling. Concepts 1 and 3 share one feedback engine (an issue router, then an outcome/IssueUpdate, then 'You said, we did') and differ only in homepage framing. All four converge on the same nav (meetings, decisions, people/bodies, get help, documents) and the same footer and alert. The real decision is not which concept. It is how much structured data secretaries will actually enter, and whether anyone will own the feedback loop.

**Recommendation**

Don't pick one of these concepts as written. Prototype one hybrid in /lab/ on plain Python data: Concept 1's plain-language, verb-led homepage on top of a deliberately thin version of Concept 4's record engine. The engine should hold only Meeting, Term and term-keyed seat assignments, plus a generated /your-reps/<constituency>/ view and a static topic router. Meetings are created by a person from a body template, never generated from a recurrence rule, because the existing facts already disagree: the live site says Mondays at 7pm, and the Jul 30 Senate meeting was a Thursday at 8pm. The only required fields on a meeting are its date, place and Box links. The homepage should show four or five real tasks, the next meeting per body with honest 'Agenda not posted' states, the reps lookup, and an expiring Notice. That hybrid survives a year of neglect and still tells the truth, which none of the four full concepts can promise.

Explicitly defer these until a named role commits in writing to maintaining them: the Decision/legislation tracker (stages and numbering are guesses until the constitution and bylaws are read), roll-call and attendance (needs a Senate rules decision), the budget and allocations explorer (needs Business Ops buy-in; budgets are 'private' in the Digital Infrastructure Plan, and the four contingency lines aren't 'where your fee goes'), every form of 'You said, we did' and issue tracking (no owner, and no peer does it, probably for that reason), and the custom Desk and Post-agenda admin views (bespoke code a volunteer team will inherit). Before any migrations, do three things. Get the constitution and RUGP readable, since the read is currently blocked, and confirm which processes really exist and who runs them. Fix import_roles and import_assignments so the constituency data from roles.csv and members.csv actually loads. Then run a timed test with a real Senate secretary entering the two existing agendas into a prototype form. If entering just the meeting and its agenda link takes more than about two minutes, the structured-record half of every concept is dead, and the site should stay a well-explained front door over Box.
