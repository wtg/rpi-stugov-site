# Rensselaer Union Constitution: Digest for the Site Redesign

Source: `docs/redesign/reference/union-constitution.tex`, last amended February 6, 2026. Amendments (appended at the end) override earlier text. Citations use "Art. [Roman] §[n]". The .tex numbers articles and sections automatically, so numbers come from file order. The amendments' own cross-references (for example "Article VIII, Section 5" for Fraternity and Sorority Life Senators) match that order. Amendments are numbered by position: I and II (2018), III (2023), IV (April 5, 2024), V (March 17, 2024, unnumbered in the file), VI (February 6, 2026). The text of Amendment III is blank in the file, so only the body text shows its effect. Arithmetic marked "derived" is ours, not the constitution's.

## 1. Bodies and officers

The Union is every enrolled Rensselaer student, graduate or undergraduate, who has paid the Union Activity Fee (Art. II). It is constituted under the authority of the Board of Trustees (Art. VII intro). It is governed by five bodies: Student Senate, Executive Board, Judicial Board, Graduate Council, Undergraduate Council.

**Union officers (Art. IV):** Grand Marshal, President of the Union, Chairperson of the Judicial Board, President of the Graduate Council, President of the Undergraduate Council. They must meet regularly to coordinate.

### Officers and others

- **Grand Marshal** (Art. IV §1): The leader and chief spokesperson for the whole Union, and the person who runs Senate meetings. Ex-officio member of all committees. Non-voting in the Senate except to break a tie. Their appointments can be nullified (see §2).
- **President of the Union** (Art. IV §2): Runs the Executive Board and is its main appointer. Ex-officio member of all Union committees. Non-voting on the Board except to break a tie. Appointments other than Executive Board members need ratification by 2/3 of the Executive Board.
- **Chairperson of the Judicial Board** (Art. IV §3): Runs the Judicial Board and proposes new members. Non-voting except to break a tie.
- **President of the Graduate Council** (Art. IV §4): Spokesperson for graduate students and leader of the Graduate Council. Ex-officio on all graduate committees. Per Amendment V, elected by the Graduate Council from among its members.
- **President of the Undergraduate Council** (Art. IV §5): Spokesperson for undergraduates and leader of the Undergraduate Council. Ex-officio on all undergraduate committees.
- **Director of the Union** (Art. V): A paid staff member who administers Union activities and funds. The Director is an adviser only and cannot veto the Executive Board. The Director may ask the Senate to begin impeachment of a Senator or Executive Board member seen as fiscally irresponsible. A majority of the Executive Board can bring charges against the Director to the Judicial Board. The Vice President for Student Life advises the Director.
- **Treasurer** (Art. VI): The Treasurer of Rensselaer holds the Union's funds and receives copies of every financial transaction.

**Parliamentary authority** (Art. VII §6): the current Robert's Rules of Order govern all subordinate bodies, unless the Constitution or a body's own special rules say otherwise.

## 2. Things a student can do

**Vote or run for office** (Art. VIII §4, §13)
- Only Union members at the time of the election may vote or run. Voting is by secret ballot.
- To be eligible a student must meet the Institute's eligibility rules plus any extra Senate regulations (Art. VIII §13). The Constitution lists no GPA or other numeric requirement.
- Grand Marshal, President of the Union, and Judicial Board Chairperson: enrolled at least one full semester (Art. IV §6, Amendment I).
- President of the Undergraduate Council: an undergraduate enrolled one full semester. President of the Graduate Council: a graduate student matriculated into a graduate program one full semester.
- Independent Senator: must not be in a fraternity or sorority. Fraternity and Sorority Life Senator: must be in one. The Director of the Union or a designee determines affiliation (Art. VIII §5).
- Limits: no running for both Grand Marshal and President of the Union (Art. IX §4). No running for more than one position on the same council (Art. IX §3). A Grand Marshal or President candidate may also run for Senator or Representative on a Class Council or the Graduate Council, but not for an officer position there (Art. IX §1). Neither may serve on the other's body, and neither may sit on a constituent council or any Union judiciary body (Art. IX §2). Term limit: no more than two full terms combined as Grand Marshal and/or President of the Union (Art. IV §6).
- Backup candidate: a "successive candidate" may run alongside a primary candidate who knows they cannot finish the term (Art. VIII §14).
- Win thresholds: Grand Marshal and President of the Union need 40% of the popular vote (Art. VIII §10). Class Council officers win by plurality. Otherwise the most votes wins (Art. VIII §8, Amendment VI). Ties are settled by a new election between the tied candidates (Art. VIII §11).
- Calendar: general elections in spring, Freshman Class Council and Freshman Senate seats in fall. Winners are installed within one week of the election's conclusion (Art. VIII §1, §3, §12). Results must be released by the end of the semester (Art. VIII §2). Terms run until the next general election for that position.
- How a name gets on the ballot, and any signature requirement, is left to the Senate (Art. VIII §6).
- Fraternity and Sorority Life Senators: three seats. Candidates are grouped by chapter. The top vote-getter wins seat 1. Seat 2 goes to the top candidate from a different grouping, or an unclassified write-in. Seat 3 goes to the top candidate from a grouping different from both earlier winners, or an unclassified write-in. If only unclassified candidates remain eligible, the highest votes win (Art. VIII §5; Amendments III, VI).

**Sign or start a petition and referendum** (Art. XII)
- A petition signed by at least 10% of all Union members forces a referendum on any matter except amending the Constitution. The Senate must hold it unless it rejects the petition by 2/3 of its total voting membership (§1).
- The Senate may also put any referendum question proposed by a Senator, committee, council or petition on the ballot by a 2/3 vote (§1).
- Timing: held within 45 days (not counting vacations) or at the next regular election, whichever comes first, and never less than 14 days after approval. It cannot happen during exam periods (§2).
- A referendum passes by majority of those voting, but only counts if at least 20% of the affected constituency votes (§3). It is binding. The Senate must enact the needed legislation. Unless the text says otherwise, only a similar referendum can rescind it (§4).

**Remove or recall an officer or senator** (Art. X)
- Grand Marshal, President of the Union, and the Presidents of the Graduate and Undergraduate Councils can be removed two ways. One is a petition demanding resignation, signed by at least 50% of their constituents. The other is a removal election in which 2/3 of the constituents voting say yes (§1).
- A removal election is valid only if at least 20% of constituents vote. It is called by a 2/3 Senate vote, or by a petition of 20% of constituents followed by a majority Senate vote (§1).
- An elected senator is recalled by 2/3 of constituents in a valid removal election. It is triggered by a petition from 20% of constituents or a majority Senate vote. Council-appointed senators are removed by 2/3 of the appointing council (§2).
- The Senate itself can remove a voting member for good cause (defined in Senate bylaws) by 2/3 of its total voting membership (derived: 20 of 29) (§2).
- Executive Board members: Senate-installed ones go by 2/3 of the Senate, or by the President's request plus a Senate majority. Council- or Senate-appointed ones go by 2/3 of the appointing body, or the President's request plus a majority of that body (§3).
- Judicial Board (or "Review Board") members can be recalled for good cause by 2/3 of the Senate (§4).
- Graduate and Class Council members can be removed for good cause (defined in bylaws) by 2/3 of that council. The person concerned cannot vote. A floor impeachment vote by majority triggers a removal vote at the next meeting (§5).
- If an appointing body leaves a seat unfilled, it forfeits that seat's vote (§2).

**Challenge an appointment to a vacant elected post** (Art. XI §5)
- If an elected position, including Union officers, is filled by appointment, a petition signed by 20% of the constituency, received by the Senate within two weeks of the announcement, forces a special election. If students are not on campus, the two weeks run from the announcement to the full campus.
- How vacancies are filled: Grand Marshal, appointed by 2/3 of the Senate within two weeks (§1). President of the Union, appointed by the Executive Board within two weeks and confirmed by 2/3 of the Senate (§2). Judicial Board Chairperson, nominated by the Judicial Board within two weeks and confirmed by 2/3 of the Senate (§3). Undergraduate Council President, elected by the Class Councils in joint session within two weeks and approved by 2/3 of the Senate (§4).

**Appointment nullification** (Art. IV §1)
- Any appointment by the Grand Marshal can be nullified by a majority of the total voting membership of the Senate (derived: 15 of 29). The Constitution does not list which appointments are covered.

**Bring a charge to the Judicial Board** (Art. VII §3)
- Any charge alleging a violation of the Constitution, the Rensselaer Student Bill of Rights, or established regulations governing student conduct may be referred to the Judicial Board. The Constitution says nothing about who may refer, how, or on what timeline.
- The Board may declare legislation of the Senate, Executive Board, Graduate Council, Undergraduate Council or Class Councils unconstitutional. It is the sole interpreter of the Constitution.
- No other board may take over this jurisdiction unless made up solely of students and clearly defined. Such a board needs consent of the Senate and the Institute President.

**Amend the Constitution** (Art. XIII)
- There is no direct student petition route. An amendment must first pass 2/3 of the Senate, then a majority of those voting in a valid constitutional election where at least 20% of eligible students vote (§1).
- One month before the student vote, the amendment goes to the Institute President, who may refer it to the Board of Trustees (§2). At least two weeks before the vote, the exact wording must be published campus-wide (§3). No votes during exam periods (§4). All amendments are recorded with the Constitution (§5).

**Get a club classified and funded**
- The Executive Board decides the classification of new organizations and changes to existing ones, "as specified in its by-laws" (Art. VII §2). It also appropriates Union funds (Art. VII §2). Application steps, classification tiers and funding criteria are not in the Constitution. Five required plus up to two optional club/organization representatives sit on the Board.

**Activity fee and class dues**
- The Senate sets the Union Activity Fee by a 2/3 vote of its total voting membership (Art. VII §1). Paying it is what makes a student a Union member (Art. II).
- Graduate class dues: changes are recommended by 2/3 of the Graduate Council, then need 2/3 of the Senate's total voting membership. The Graduate Council controls spending (Art. VII §4).
- Undergraduate class dues: changes are recommended by majority of the Undergraduate Council, then need 2/3 of the Senate's total voting membership. Each Class Council recommends spending by majority, subject to majority approval of the Undergraduate Council (Art. VII §5).

**Serve on Board of Trustees committees**
- The Senate "shall establish procedures for selection and removal" of student members of Board of Trustees committees and of all other appointments (Art. VII §1). The Constitution gives no committee list, seat count, or eligibility rule.

**Ask for a change in your representation**
- The Senate is responsible for "proportionate representation" of all Union members on the Senate, both Councils, the Executive Board and the Judicial Board, and must give adequate notice of upcoming elections (Art. VIII §6).

## 3. Transparency and named records

- **The budget and records of all additional expenditures and appropriations are public documents** (Art. VII §2, item 4). This is the strongest explicit transparency rule in the text.
- The Treasurer receives copies of all financial transactions (Art. VI). No money may be spent by any committee, officer or member without an Executive Board requisition or an authorized agent (Art. XIV).
- Campus-wide publication of the exact amendment wording at least two weeks before a vote (Art. XIII §3). Adequate notice of elections (Art. VIII §6). A record of all amendments is appended to the Constitution (Art. XIII §5).
- Senators must report regularly to their respective councils (Art. VII §1).
- **Named records:** the Union Constitution; bylaws of the Senate, Executive Board, Judicial Board, Graduate Council and Undergraduate Council; the Rensselaer Student Bill of Rights; Senate election procedure and regulations; the Institute's eligibility rules; Robert's Rules of Order. **"RUGP" does not appear in the Constitution.** The site should not describe it from this source. The Constitution does not say where any of these records are published, and does not require meeting minutes or agendas to be public.

## 4. Not settled by the Constitution (do not state on the site as constitutional)

- Quorum for any body (only a Graduate Council quorum adjustment is mentioned, Art. VII §4).
- The amount of the Union Activity Fee, how it is collected, refunds, or waivers. Class dues amounts and who pays.
- Club classification tiers, funding criteria, application procedure, budget deadlines, and the size of the budget.
- Judicial Board procedure: who may file, timelines, penalties, appeals, hearings, and what the "Review Board" in Art. X §4 is.
- Ballot access: signature thresholds or deadlines for candidates (Senate decides), campaign rules, election dates, and the extra Senate eligibility regulations.
- Good-cause standards for removing members (left to Senate, Graduate Council and Undergraduate Council bylaws).
- Which Grand Marshal appointments exist and how many, and the list of Board of Trustees committees and student seats.
- Committee structures of the Senate and Executive Board, and the Senate meeting schedule.
- What happens if no Grand Marshal or President candidate reaches 40%. The text only handles ties (Art. VIII §10, §11). Whether the Graduate Council President is elected by all graduate students or by the Council: Amendment V says the Council elects from its members, while Art. VIII §1 and Art. X §1 still treat the post as a general-election position with "constituents".
- Text conflicts to avoid repeating: Graduate Senators are "six" in Amendment I but "seven" in the current body text (use seven, which matches 29 Senate seats). Art. VIII §12(g) refers to an "eleven member Graduate Council", which predates the current 12-plus. At-large Graduate Council members are "elected at large" (Art. VII §4) but selected from applicants by the Council (Art. VIII §12(g)). Art. XIII §1 says "2/3 of the Student Senate", not "total voting membership". Some titles still say "Chairman" (Art. XI §3).
- Art. V says the Director's overreach is handled by Executive Board charges, but the Constitution does not give a hiring or firing process for the Director beyond Executive Board approval of "hiring and continuance of all administrative personnel" (Art. VII §2).

## 5. Plain-language glossary

- **Union / Rensselaer Union:** All students who have paid the Union Activity Fee, and the organization they form.
- **Student Senate:** The 29-voting-member body that makes rules, sets the fee, and approves many appointments.
- **Executive Board (E-Board):** The group that builds and approves the Union budget, gives out money and classifies clubs.
- **Judicial Board:** The student court for rights and conduct cases that also decides what the Constitution means.
- **Graduate Council / Undergraduate Council:** The advocacy bodies for graduate and undergraduate students.
- **Class Council:** The elected group that represents one undergraduate class and recommends spending its class dues.
- **Grand Marshal:** The Union's top spokesperson, who runs the Senate and votes only to break ties.
- **President of the Union:** The head of the Executive Board, who appoints most of its members.
- **Director of the Union:** A paid staff adviser who runs Union activities and funds but cannot veto the Executive Board.
- **Treasurer:** The Rensselaer Treasurer, who holds Union funds and sees every financial transaction.
- **Union Activity Fee:** The fee that makes you a Union member, with an amount set by 2/3 of the Senate.
- **Class dues:** Money collected from members of a class, spent by that class's council under Undergraduate or Graduate Council oversight.
- **Referendum:** A vote of students on a question, triggered by Senate approval or a 10% petition; binding if at least 20% of the affected students vote.
- **Petition:** A signed request from students (10%, 20% or 50% depending on the goal) that forces the Senate or an election to act.
- **Recall / removal election:** A student vote to remove an elected officer or senator mid-term.
- **Impeachment:** A council's floor vote that starts the process of removing a member for good cause.
- **Ex-officio:** A seat you hold because of your office, not by separate election.
- **Quorum:** The minimum number of members who must be present for a body to act. The Constitution does not set it for most bodies.
- **Plurality:** The most votes, even without a majority.
- **Ratify / confirm:** To formally approve someone else's appointment.
- **Nullify:** To cancel an appointment after it was made, which the Senate can do to the Grand Marshal's appointments.
- **Valid election:** One where enough students vote (usually 20%) for the result to count.
- **By-laws:** A body's own detailed operating rules, passed under the Constitution.
- **Student Bill of Rights:** The Rensselaer document the Judicial Board can enforce, which is not reproduced in the Constitution.
